last year, during [[ipc_experiments_week-4]] (week 4 of physical-computing), i remember mustering up the courage, walking up to [[nikolai]] & asking him to help me understand what a transistor did. 

he patiently did so: 

![[z_images/IMG_6459.webp|380]]

i remember thinking: "i want to be like him when i'm a second-year". 

in fact, it was because of more interactions like these that i joined the shop (see [[shop-staff application letter]]). 

---

in a conversation with [[aram]] this semester, we observed that no one was here during the motor lab. no one had fried their arduinos, and no one seemed to care about how transistors worked. 

[[james]] mentioned how no one booked a single coding lab appointment this year. i remember how much time we all spent helping each other debug — code or circuits — at all hours of the day. 

the only interactions i had this year to help someone (apart from when it was physical fabrication) was when their submission was due the next day, and their agents weren't able to help them figure it out. *that's* when they needed a human: to make their project work for one glorious day. 

---

how strange is it that lesser people care about how things work anymore. even in itp, somehow, it is a perfectly acceptable response to say "i don't really know how it works" when showing a piece of programmatic work. 

often, people will counter me with: "oh, it's making the barrier to creative expression easier". sure, but you could do that home. to me atleast, it was lovely to see [[justina]] spend a year obsessing over stepper-motors. or [[gabriel]] spending nights just to get the perfect beep-boop-bop with two oscillators. 

people [[gabriel]] who stayed up many nights making two oscillators make beep-boop-bop sounds. or staying up all night to solder 37 leds, wiring them up with multiplexors, and bit-banging to them just to share a [newfound obsession with electrons](https://arjunmakesthings.github.io/projects/2025_little-green-things/page.html).  

---

i hope people obsess over transistors again; 
or curly brackets & semicolons; 
and non-entering if-statements; 
and leds; 
and stumble upon bit-banging; 
or blow up a few potentiometers & capacitors. 

make the place feel lively again.
