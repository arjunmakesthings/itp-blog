ask: 
make 5 of something that are identical ([[people/phil|phil]] said exactly the same in class). 

i wanted to make 5 of something that, together, are more than the individual parts. i also wanted them to move. 

---
realised that physical-making is best thought about *while* making, and not in my head. so, i began prototyping. 

i wanted objects (cubes) in a grid to move, and a connection between them to change. the connection would be the image, and the cubes would be supporting-materials. 

![[z_images/IMG_6270.mov]]

![[z_images/IMG_6281.mov]]

messed around with half-cuts, some ikea-inspired mechanisms, et-cetera for the center piece. 

![[z_images/IMG_6267.jpg]]

![[z_images/IMG_6268.jpg]]

![[z_images/IMG_6269.mov]]

[[people/ian|ian]] helped me understand that i was obsessing over the wrong thing (mechanism over replication), and that wood probably wasn't the best material for something that moves. <mark>wood is best for something solid</mark>.

---
grappled with that for a few days, came in and made a few sketches. 

![[z_images/IMG_6300.jpg]]

[[people/ian|ian]] had recommended [tinkercad](https://www.tinkercad.com) to help see things in 3-d. worked more, to visualise a simple thing made of 5 smaller things that move. 

![[z_images/Screenshot 2025-09-19 at 10.42.32.png]]

i wanted each frame to pivot, but someone at the shop helped me see that it isn't possible (without more complex mechanisms). so, i reduced my expectations, and focused on something that rotates on the pivot point that connects the frames.
