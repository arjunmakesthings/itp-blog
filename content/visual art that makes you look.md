this weekend, i visited the [krasner & pollock exhibit](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/krasner-and-pollock-past-continuous) at the met. 

i questioned what about pollock's art speaks to me, because i went with someone to whom it didn't. they were used to more sensorial-stimulation; for me the visual depth was enough. 

what is common across pollock's work & [[notes from light exhibition by zach lieberman]], is that ==they keep you looking== (perhaps what [[tom-igoe]] calls 'sustained' interaction (albeit this isn't 'interactive')). i stood in front of this for a while: 

![[IMG_8642.webp|331]]

the figure — what seems to me like a lady, in a dynamic pose; with eyes around her; there is so much to discover in the painting. as you keep looking, more ==details are revealed to you (they emerge from what wasn't)==. 

that is the thing about complexity — ==structure emerges when you look==.

---

the other bit of reflection worth articulating about is that cubist painters were pushing the visual definition of a form — what does it mean to represent something? 

look at this pollock sketch: 

![[IMG_8640.jpg|395]]

it such a beautiful exploration of form — what can the human head look like? i don't know the exact question he was trying to explore, but the sheet reveals that he was trying to find a form; ==a way to look at something in a way that hasn't been done before==. 

that is also valuable.

---

can i make people look? can i make people go beyond seeing — go to noticing; identify structures at play; ignite curiosity such that they may think of the world in similar ways? 

i think that's what i'm trying to do with my thesis.
