[[anh]] gave a presentation on how the [pisa study](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/pisa.html)  mislabels children's dreams about the future to make categorization easier. for example: a kid who says "i want to be president" gets filed under "legislators and senior officials". 

and then she found a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RUup841pZrs) where kids *actually* say what they want to become, unfiltered. she then makes the case that the scope for a 'valid' answer keeps shrinking as you grow older (dinosaur -> fairy -> job position). 

---

anyhow, that got me thinking about how we ask people: "what do you want to make of yourself" or "what do you want to make of your life". i have a conversation tonight with a younger designer, and i anticipated myself asking this question. 

i think this question is wrong. ==no one can decide what they want to make of their life, at a period in time.== i've seen so many non-linear trajectories now — so very many — that it's clear to me that we should be asking: ==what do you want to do next?== because the answer to that is motivated by their current curiosities. 

also makes me think [[curiosity is a luxury]].
