as the final deliverable for this course, students are asked to write an essay. i was confused enough to ask *why* in class, since the reason wasn't mentioned anywhere on the syllabus. [[people/sharon de la cruz|sharon de la cruz]] said: 

> to get people to reflect on making methodology; be aware of the context; and practice communication (of the experiment we'd conduct). the objective is to give people a chance to 'scratch their curiosity'. 

brief is [here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1gujBYcYgiLLqOXvqVz9cZBQiIYnuTrGZq1umFxFr8iA/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.v6o4fbr5cvc8).

i had already done [something like this](https://arjunmakesthings.github.io/projects/2021_pui-vs-gui/page.html) in undergraduate-study (paper [here](https://arjunmakesthings.github.io/projects/2021_pui-vs-gui/Quantitative%20Comparison%20Between%20A%20Traditional%20User%20Interface%20(GUI)%20and%20A%20Perceptual%20User%20Interface%20(PUI).pdf)). so, i used this opportunity to explore a few lines of thought: 
1. <mark>a creative-practitioner works with feelings</mark>. they are — both — working material and a parameter to gauge success in communication. 
2. <mark>when making big things & moving fast</mark>, like we do at itp, <mark>it is easy to forget that little decisions compound into big differences</mark> between intended communication of feelings, and the feelings that are ultimately perceived. 
3. to (experimentally) prove these differences, i wish to explore the <mark>differences in how a written piece of communication in english feels, when read in different cases</mark> (letter-case, uppercase, lowercase). 
4. i've been <mark>wanting to <a href = "https://arjunmakesthings.github.io/notes.html">write</a> a piece on why i write in lowercase letters</mark>. it was originally influenced by herbert bayer's *[why we write everything in small letters](https://www.bauhaus-bookshelf.org/bauhaus_journals_1926_1931-1.html)* & [shobhan](https://www.setwrite.in/reflections/201602-yet-another-tirade-against-the-uppercase.html), and remained as i saw the differences in the perceiver's feelings when they received my messages (in comparison to the same message sent in sentence-case).

# with devan, for bauhaus 1.1926 p. 6: 
why do we (bauhaus school) write everything small? 

it's inconsequential to write differently than we speak. we don't speak with any uppercase sounds; so we also don't write with them. and isn't it so that you say the same thing with one set of alphabet than with two sets of alphabets? why does one combine two alphabets with two different characters in one word and, thereby, make the writing in the same typeface more unharmonious. 

whether large or small, the large alphabet is unreadable in mid-sentence; therefore, the small.  in the case of a typewriter, the limitation of using only small letters means greater relief / relaxation / ease / efficiency (save time). when we think further,
