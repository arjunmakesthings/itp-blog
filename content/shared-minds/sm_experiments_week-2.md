building on thoughts from the reading (watching (?)), i am restricted to my viewpoints that originated in the reading from [[sm_experiments_week-1]]. the methods we use to study consciousness — right now — are unverifiable; i.e. everyone can claim to have their own experience, and we just won't be able to assess otherwise.

therefore, the parallels drawn between human-thinking & machine-thinking — to me — seem too timid. 

i see all these questions that you pose, but fail to arrive at any sort of shareable thought. neural-networks are probabilistic-machines; and i'm just not sure our brains are the same. do we have weighted networks for all the perceptual stimuli that we experience? i don't know; i just don't know. 

i remember some video from dwarkesh where they were discussing the hugging-face ai-hack. i also read some stuff about jacob coxon's warnings about a.i., and why he quit anthropic before the big payout. i mean, i've been collecting a list of incidents against a.i.: [[case against ai]]. 

yeah, i'm just rambling. ==i don't have anything useful to say about the topic — i don't know enough.== what i 'think' is baseless; and strongly opinionated by feelings i cannot justifiably defend. maybe it'll take over the world; maybe it'll kill us all; maybe i'm dumb; maybe i'm 'backward'; maybe — like [[aram]] said — "i'm a calligrapher fighting against the printing-press, and am doomed to be defeated". to be honest: thinking about a.i. brings with it some sort of existential doom. all of us had access to large-language-models for last week's assignment. we were supposed to make tools to express consciousness — that should have been a cool thing, no? someone in that class should have gone like: "wow, that was cool — i want to use it". no one did. we didn't even look at assignments intently. when everyone is a maker, who sees the work then? who are we making for? as a maker, we're extinct. why would you trust me to make a better tool for you when you *think* you can make a good one for yourself? i'm dead as a designer. i'm also dead as a creative-technologist — you don't need me to prototype anymore. i'm dead as a creative-thinking-partner — a.i. has more context about the world than i do (is what you think). i'm dead as a teacher, because a.i. will confidently guide you down a path, and leave no room for struggle. i'm dead as a peer, because you have a large-language-echo-chamber that will reaffirm your thoughts (as opposed to disagreeing with you). 

a.i. hasn't biologically killed me yet; but it sure has killed the world that was. 

whatever. i'm supposed to just make for myself? i don't know.
