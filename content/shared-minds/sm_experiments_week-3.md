# writing: 
> asks:  
> 
> **left:** 
> If you are collaborating with someone, which would you rather receive: Google Doc or a screenshot, Illustrator file or a JPEG, MIDI or MP3? Which enables a conversation instead of just a statement? How does breaking things into addressable and rearrangeable parts enable expression? Is it too much “paint by numbers” for your taste?
>
> What are some systems that have used this technique of a discrete combinatorial (Pinker) or Universal System (Deutsch), and what were the results? How does it create a vast “space” of expression?
>
> This obviously works for words (and numbers and DNA), but what about images? Imagine a scene, and then decide what are the changeable parts and how do you create a syntax for them to go well together? Can you create a visual language? Can you imagine a medium like film except “unflatten” with the parts still changeable?
> 
> **right:**
> How much of your knowledge is explicit? McGilchrist’s description of the two different styles of understanding the world, left brain and right brain, he says the right brain way of processing the world is often undervalued but enables most of the good things in life, like humor and empathy. Do you buy that?
>
> How well has computation done working with either style? Has computation furthered the personal and societal injuries of an imbalanced emphasis on a style of breaking things down into recombinable parts that we celebrated by Deutsch and Pinker?
>
> Will Machine Learning’s ability to capture beyond our explicit “if statements” and find myriad correlations of correlations allow computers to work more in the holistic style of the right brain? LLMs know the relationship between words, but the world is not made out of words. Can diffusion models know the relationship between pixels? Does this bring us closer to applying computation on a blended system?
>
> Can machine learning training find the important recombinable features for us but then render them out into convincing wholes to give us the best of both halves?
