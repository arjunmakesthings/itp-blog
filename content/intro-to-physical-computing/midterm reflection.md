i thought i failed the ask, but i didn't. 

granted, i was not able to achieve the desired 'interactivity' (due to my misdoings with the fsr), but i: 

- made a system with physical controls.
- kept people engaged. 
- understood all the technical concepts. 
- made people enjoy. 

i do <mark>wish that i worked in a team</mark>. i was envious of seeing people <mark>split up their work</mark>, and <mark>achieve something greater than what they could individually</mark> produce. 

i regret not scaling down my project. that prevented my experimentation with testing, physical-interaction-design, and actually being able to see how people interacted with the thing i made. in the next one, i shall scale it down — to be able to <mark>do a small thing really well</mark>. it would help to account for equal amounts of time for all the things we must do (and not let circuitry take up all my time). 

---
throughout this midterm-period, i've managed to <mark>help multiple people with their circuits & their programs</mark>. that, to me, was a <mark>richer learning experience than making my own project</mark>. it <mark>forced me to grapple with the basics</mark>, and do so with a high level of confidence since someone else was desperately relying on me. 

i am happy with the extra hours that i invested at the beginning of this course. this is clear evidence for me to <mark>go about everything in my life with high amounts of curiosity</mark> — i am here to learn.
